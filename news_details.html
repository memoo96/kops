Thamer Al-Issa to *Al-Jarida*: Dental care is essential for osteoporosis patients.

Dr. Thamer Al-Eisa, Consultant and Head of the Endocrinology and Diabetes Unit at Jaber Hospital and Vice President of the Kuwait Osteoporosis Society, emphasized that collaboration between the treating dentist and the physician managing osteoporosis is crucial for providing optimal patient care—particularly for osteoporosis patients requiring oral surgery. Speaking to *Al-Jarida*, Dr. Al-Eisa noted that osteoporosis is a chronic condition requiring treatment to prevent fractures and associated complications. He explained that various treatments are available to maintain or increase bone density; prominent among these are bisphosphonates and denosumab, both of which have been used for many years. He added that patients undergoing these treatments are advised to maintain good oral and dental hygiene to prevent a specific side effect: jawbone necrosis. He noted that this condition is rare; it involves the jawbone losing its ability to heal, resulting in a large, unhealed wound where the bone becomes exposed—uncovered by oral tissue—leading to microbial infections, pain, and swelling. Consequently, jawbone fractures may occur, and the infection becomes difficult to treat. He further explained that while jawbone necrosis is painful and can worsen over time, it may sometimes present without symptoms and typically resolves with appropriate treatment. He noted that poor oral and dental hygiene, along with continued smoking, are additional contributing factors to the development of jawbone necrosis. He emphasized the importance of maintaining good oral and dental hygiene while undergoing treatment with bisphosphonates or denosumab, as well as visiting the dentist every six months—or as recommended by the treating dentist. Routine dental care—such as cleanings, fillings, and root canal treatments—should continue as usual without the need to interrupt osteoporosis therapy. He added that if oral or maxillofacial surgery is required, it is preferable to complete the procedure before initiating these osteoporosis treatments. Al-Issa further noted that some cancer patients use bisphosphonates or denosumab to manage the disease's impact on the bones; these are often administered intravenously at higher doses or with greater frequency than in osteoporosis cases, placing these patients at higher risk of jawbone necrosis. Consequently, it is crucial for cancer patients receiving these therapies to undergo a comprehensive dental examination, obtain jawbone X-rays, and complete any necessary jaw surgery before starting treatment. He stated that if a surgical procedure becomes necessary after treatment with bisphosphonates or denosumab has begun, it is advisable to pause the medication until the oral or dental surgery is complete and to resume treatment only after the surgical site has fully healed—typically within one or two months following the procedure.
